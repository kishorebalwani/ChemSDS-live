What is a Non-Dangerous Goods (NDG) Declaration?

A Non-Dangerous Goods (NDG) Declaration Letter (also called a Non-Hazardous Certificate) is a legally binding statement issued on the shipper's company letterhead. It certifies that a cargo or parcel consignment does not contain substances classified under IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations (DGR) or the IMDG Code.

International courier services such as FedEx, DHL Express, Blue Dart, UPS, and Aramex routinely demand an NDG letter along with a matching 16-section Safety Data Sheet (SDS) before accepting liquids, powders, cosmetics, herbal extracts, essential oils, or battery items.

What Is the Full Form of NDG in Shipping?

NDG stands for Non-Dangerous Goods. In shipping and logistics, NDG refers to goods that are not classified as Dangerous Goods under applicable transport regulations.

A Non-Dangerous Goods (NDG) Declaration may be requested by air carriers, shipping lines, or freight forwarders to support the transport classification of a consignment. Classification should always be supported by appropriate product information, specifically Section 14 (Transport Information) of an SDS.

NDG vs DG: What Is the Difference?

  • Dangerous Goods (DG): Substances, materials, or articles that pose a hazard during transport and are classified under applicable regulations (such as IATA DGR or IMDG Code, Classes 1 through 9).
  • Non-Dangerous Goods (NDG): Goods that do not meet the applicable criteria for classification as Dangerous Goods during the intended mode of transport.

Top Reasons Couriers Hold or Reject NDG Letters

  • No Matching SDS Attached: Courier safety desks (like DHL DG desk or FedEx DG Hotline) will reject an NDG declaration if it is not supported by a valid 16-section GHS Safety Data Sheet.
  • Missing AWB or Invoice Number: The letter must explicitly link to the physical package tracking or Airway Bill (AWB) number.
  • Conflict in Section 14 of SDS: If your SDS Section 14 shows a UN number (like UN 1993 or UN 1169) but your letter claims "Non-Dangerous", the parcel is flagged immediately for misdeclaration.
  • Not on Official Company Letterhead: Plain printouts without company stamp, registration details, and authorized signature are rejected by port customs.
