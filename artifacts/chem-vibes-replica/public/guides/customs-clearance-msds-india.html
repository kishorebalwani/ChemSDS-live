Every year, Indian chemical manufacturers, merchant exporters, and traders face thousands of dollars in port demurrage and container detention fees. In the majority of cases, the hold is triggered not by the cargo itself, but by discrepancies between the Shipping Bill / Bill of Lading and the product's Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS).

Top 4 Reasons Why Indian Customs Reject MSDS

  • Legacy 8 or 9-Section Formats: Customs authorities strictly mandate the international 16-section GHS format (UN GHS Rev. 10). Outdated MSDS sheets are rejected on sight.
  • Vague Section 14 Classification: Declaring a product as generic "Non-Hazardous" without flash point data, UN designation, or technical justification is a major trigger for cargo hold.
  • Missing Flash Point & Physical Properties: For liquids, essential oils, and solvents, Section 9 must clearly state the flash point (°C closed cup) to confirm DG vs. Non-DG status.
  • No 24/7 Emergency Contact: International freight rules require an active, responsive emergency contact number clearly visible on page 1 of the sheet.

Section 14: What Customs & CHAs Examine

Section 14 is the primary focus of port surveyors, airline cargo acceptance desks, and Custom House Agents (CHAs). Ensure these fields are explicitly documented:

Requirement Dangerous Goods (DG Cargo) Non-Dangerous Goods (NDG)
UN Number Assigned 4-digit code (e.g., UN 1993) Designated as "Not Applicable / Not Regulated"
Proper Shipping Name Exact UN Transport specification Commercial / Chemical trade name
Transport Hazard Class Class 1 through 9 (e.g., Class 3, Class 8) Non-hazardous for transport
Packing Group PG I, PG II, or PG III Not Applicable
Marine Pollutant Yes / No (Based on Section 12 data) No
Air Cargo (IATA) Cargo Aircraft Only (CAO) or Passenger limit Safe for passenger and cargo aircraft

Sea Freight (JNPT, Mundra, Hazira) vs. Air Cargo Shipments

Ocean Shipments (IMDG Code): Shipping lines require the SDS alongside the Dangerous Goods Declaration (DGD). Marine surveyors verify Flash Point, Packing Group, and Marine Pollutant data to ensure safe container stowage on the vessel.

Air Cargo (IATA DGR): Major air carriers (IndiGo CarGo, Emirates SkyCargo, Air India Cargo, Qatar Airways) inspect chemical goods with zero margin for error. If shipping liquids, physical parameters like vapor pressure and boiling point must be consistent throughout the document.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can ChemSDS coordinate directly with my CHA or freight forwarder?

Yes. If your Custom House Agent or airline freight coordinator requests specific wording, UN numbers, or flash point disclosures, we can incorporate the necessary compliance parameters into the sheet.

What is an NDG Declaration and do I need one?

A Non-Dangerous Goods (NDG) declaration confirms that your product does not fall under UN Class 1 to 9. Airlines and ports often require both an NDG declaration certificate and a compliant 16-section SDS showing "Not Regulated" in Section 14.
